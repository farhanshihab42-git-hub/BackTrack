# BackTrack Requirements

## Purpose

BackTrack supports a 12-week home-exercise rehabilitation study for people with low back pain. It records adherence, symptoms, outcomes, and authorized clinical review. The clinical exercise protocol and study procedures are supplied and approved by the research team; the application must not invent or prescribe treatment.

## User Roles

The application has three interfaces backed by one secured service and database:

- **Patient:** access only to the signed-in patient's own profile, assigned program, check-ins, reports, history, and approved educational materials.
- **Physiotherapist:** access only to patients assigned or otherwise authorized to that therapist.
- **Administrator/Researcher:** restricted study and administration access, provisioned for approved study personnel. Use pseudonymous participant IDs by default and separate research-analysis permissions from user administration and identifiable clinical-record access.

Role selection in a demo UI is not authentication or access control.

## Patient Requirements

- Register through the study-approved enrollment process and connect with a therapist through an invitation code, approved therapist selection, or another approved mechanism.
- View and manage permitted profile details and the assigned 12-week program.
- View only physiotherapist-approved exercises; patients cannot change prescriptions.
- Record daily exercise adherence, pain and symptom reports, and complete assigned baseline/follow-up outcome questionnaires.
- Review personal exercise history and progress, and access study-approved educational materials.
- See the current date after signing in.
- Never access another participant's information.

## Physiotherapist Requirements

- Register subject to verification or administrator approval.
- Manage an authorized patient list; access must be limited to assigned/authorized patients.
- Review assessments, adherence, pain and disability outcomes, and safety reports.
- Record severity level and McKenzie directional preference (flexion, extension, or lateral).
- Select from approved, preconfigured protocols. The severity and directional-preference selections determine the applicable approved protocol and patient-facing program. Protocol contents and severity mappings are supplied by the study team, not generated by the application.
- Document clinical decisions and program modifications with an audit trail.

## Administrator/Researcher Requirements

- View recruitment and participant status, adherence across participants, baseline/follow-up completion, pain/disability trends, missing data, missed assessments, and adverse-event reports.
- Generate descriptive statistics and research charts.
- Export authorized research datasets to CSV with required fields and no unnecessary direct identifiers.
- Manage study configuration and approved exercise-library content only when granted the relevant permission.
- Do not silently change historical adherence records or clinical outcomes.

## Data Model

Link the following records under access controls and an audit trail:

- Participant ID and patient account
- Assigned physiotherapist and authorization/assignment history
- Approved exercise prescription, protocol version, and modifications
- Dated adherence records
- Pain and symptom measurements
- Disability/outcome questionnaire responses and completion status
- Clinical assessments/reviews and decisions
- Adverse-event/safety reports
- Consent, study status, and baseline/follow-up schedule as required by the approved study

Keep direct identifiers separate from the analytical dataset wherever practical. Record sensitive record access, exports, prescription changes, permission changes, and record modifications.

## Authentication, Authorization, and Data Protection

- Use secure password storage and appropriate session management.
- Do not allow public registration to grant privileged roles. Physiotherapist and administrator/researcher access requires approval/provisioning.
- Enforce authorization on every relevant backend endpoint and database operation; frontend route hiding is not sufficient.
- Verify patient ownership and therapist assignment on every data access. Revoked accounts and permissions must stop working immediately.
- Use multi-factor authentication for privileged accounts where feasible.
- Only authorized users may export data. Follow approved consent, ethics, security, retention, and data-protection requirements.
- The backend and database must be selected and configured for the institution's deployment and study requirements before real participant data is used.

## Acceptance Tests

- A patient cannot retrieve another patient's records or modify an approved prescription.
- A physiotherapist cannot access patients outside their authorization.
- An unapproved account cannot access privileged interfaces or APIs, and public registration cannot grant a privileged role.
- The researcher can retrieve only the authorized study dataset; exports contain required fields without unnecessary identifiers.
- Adherence calculations match underlying scheduled sessions and adherence records.
- Sensitive access, exports, prescription changes, and record modifications are audited.
- Patient records remain available after logout and subsequent login.
- Revoked accounts and permissions no longer provide access.

## Current Prototype Boundary

The current BackTrack app is a front-end demonstration using generated sample records and browser `localStorage`. It has no authentication, shared backend/database, server-enforced role authorization, approval workflow, audit trail, real CSV export, or production data protection. Its role/view controls must not be represented as secure access control. Do not enter identifiable or real patient data, and do not use the prototype for clinical decisions.

## Decisions Needed Before Study Implementation

- Approved severity categories and the exact mapping from severity plus McKenzie preference to the study's exercise protocols.
- The selected outcome questionnaire(s), permitted implementation, scoring, and baseline/follow-up schedule.
- Approved registration, therapist verification, patient-enrollment, and account-revocation procedures.
- Institutional deployment, database, hosting, consent, ethics, retention, and data-protection requirements.
- Which study personnel can access identifiable clinical records, administer users, configure content, and export analytical data.

## Supplied Protocol Draft (proposal demo only)

The supplied `BackTrack_LBP_Severity_Protocol.docx` is identified as v0.3 draft and requests review by a licensed physiotherapist before any patient use. It is the source for the following proposal-demo behavior, not an approved clinical standard:

- ODI is calculated from ten section scores (sum divided by answered sections × 5); exactly one omitted/not-applicable section uses 45 as the denominator. Fewer than nine answered sections cannot be scored.
- Score bands: 61–80% Programme A (Crippled); 41–60% B (Severe); 21–40% C (Moderate); 0–20% D (Minimal); 81–100% not enrolled.
- The draft includes programme-specific exercises, dosage, weekly schedule, and optional modalities. It originally describes patient-selected movement preference and ODI-based programme assignment.
- The draft proposes a 12-week course, at least two weeks in A/B/C, and progression after the last three completed sessions have every required exercise ticked, the stated pain-improvement condition is met, and post-session pain has not risen by two or more points. D continues until week 12.
- The patient demo records exercise ticks, pain before/after, daily warning-sign checks, session adherence, baseline and end-of-course ODI. It does not reproduce ODI item wording or provide media files.
- A checked urgent warning sign blocks exercise recording and shows the draft's stop/seek-care message. The in-app checklist is not clinical triage.
- The source protocol describes therapist assessment as outside the app, while the later UI guidelines require an in-app physiotherapist workflow to choose severity and McKenzie preference.

## Later UI workflow direction

The user clarified that the physiotherapist should choose the severity programme and directional preference, and requested simulated Patient, Physiotherapist, and Developer/Researcher views. The current proposal demo follows that workflow: ODI is displayed as a baseline/reference measure; the physiotherapist demo view assigns a preset programme and direction; the patient view displays the assigned plan read-only. This is a prototype-specific workflow choice that differs from the original draft's automatic ODI assignment and patient-selected direction, and should be confirmed in the clinically reviewed study protocol.

The Developer/Researcher view includes a CSV export with pseudonymous synthetic IDs only. None of the role views are authenticated, and this export is not an authorized or production research export.

The UI and stored demo values are synthetic. Do not use the implemented calculations or safety language to guide patient care until the protocol and its rules are reviewed and approved by qualified study personnel and the required institutional review is complete. Interpretations left ambiguous in the source, including scheduling and adherence conventions, must be confirmed before a study implementation.

## Current protocol-demo implementation boundary

The current app has a patient proposal flow and a synthetic study overview, persisted in browser `localStorage`. It does not implement the target authenticated patient/physiotherapist/administrator system, server-side authorization, therapist review, or database described above. Client-side view switching remains demonstration-only.
